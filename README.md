I came to psychology through a strange route — forensic sciences first,
then IT, then the clinic. Somewhere along the way I stopped being able
to separate the two questions: what does a structure do to the people
inside it, and what does the psyche do with the structures it's given?

So now that's the work. I'm a clinical psychology researcher (MA,
Istanbul & Berlin), trained in psychoanalytic psychopathology and
projective methods — Rorschach, TAT — and I use them to study
environments nobody designed a projective test for: algorithmic feeds,
surveillance systems, the physical internet itself. Undersea cables
and data centers are psychic objects too; they just don't get read
that way.

The other thread is critical, liberation, and decolonial psychology:
what happens when structural problems get translated into individual
pathology, and what a psychoanalysis might look like that refuses
that translation. Some of this becomes papers. Some of it becomes
video essays. Some of it just becomes a folder of notes in CryptPad,
because I'd rather my thinking live on tools that don't watch me
think.

### What I'm working on

- **Digital infrastructure & power** — mapping physical internet
  infrastructure, surveillance models, and the socio-technical systems
  that shape agency.
- **Critical, liberation & decolonial psychology** — the psychologization
  of structural problems, hermeneutical injustice, and what psychoanalysis
  looks like when it stops treating the political as noise.
- **Projective methods** — Rorschach and TAT research on algorithmic
  environments and subjectivation.
- **Video essays** — scripting long-form pieces on tech infrastructure,
  surveillance, and everyday techniques of control.
- **Open & private tools** — decentralized, privacy-focused software
  (CryptPad and friends) as alternative digital spaces.
